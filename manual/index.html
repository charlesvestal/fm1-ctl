Manual

CTL-1 turns your M-VAVE FM-1 into a MIDI controller. Play notes on the keys in any scale, with its chords under the black keys. Send controllers from the knobs, in four banks. Run an arpeggiator and a four-track Euclidean drum sequencer on its clock. Everything goes out over USB and Bluetooth, and MIDI from the TRS jack is passed along. The FM-1 also plays it all on its own General MIDI sounds, so it works with nothing connected. If you like, the keys can sense how hard you play them, for velocity and aftertouch.

1Install

To try CTL-1 first, open the CTL-1 emulator. It runs the same code in your browser, with sound, and you can play it with a mouse, a touch screen or the computer keyboard. In Chrome or Edge, it can send to your MIDI ports too.

To install it, open the web installer in Chrome or Edge. Connect the FM-1 with a USB data cable and press Install. Don't unplug it while it's writing. When it's done, the FM-1 restarts into CTL-1.

To go back to M-VAVE's firmware, use "Back to the stock firmware" at the bottom of the same page. Download the FM-1 V15 file from the link there, choose it and press the button. M-VAVE's own updater, M-UPGRADE, works too.

Installing other firmware is at your own risk. If the FM-1 ever won't start, see Recovering an FM-1 that won't start.

Install from the command line

Download the firmware file (ctl-VERSION.fwsc) from the releases page and get the CTL-1 source from GitHub. Install Python 3 with pip3 install mido python-rtmidi, then run:

python3 base/tools/fm1_install.py ctl-VERSION.fwsc

2Getting started

  1. Connect. Plug the FM-1 into a computer with a USB data cable. It shows up as a MIDI device named "CTL-1 FM-1". A Mac that knew the FM-1 with other firmware may keep the old name until you remove it in Audio MIDI Setup.
  2. Play. The keys play notes from F3, and the FM-1 plays them too, on a piano. Press OCT− or OCT+ to move the keys an octave.
  3. Turn KNOB 1–4. On the Play page they send four controllers: cutoff, resonance, modulation and volume. Press FX for the next of four banks.
  4. Pick a sound. Turn PRESETS to send a program change, and the FM-1's own sound follows it. Turn ALGORITHM to set the MIDI channel.
  5. Choose a scale. Press SEL, then turn KNOB 2. Now the white keys play the scale and the black keys play its chords.
  6. Arpeggiate. Press ARP and turn KNOB 1 to On, then hold a chord.
  7. Add drums. Press SEQ and PLAY to start four Euclidean drum tracks on channel 10. Turn KNOB 1–4 to shape the selected track.

CTL-1 saves your settings by itself a few seconds after you change something.

Each page has its own button, and that button lights while you're on the page.

4The screen

  • The top bar shows the page's name, and on Play, the knob bank's letter in a chip. The MIDI channel is in the middle, and the tempo with a play triangle is on the right. The triangle is dark while playing and flashes on each beat. When the clock comes from outside, "EXT" replaces the tempo. A dot at the far right means there are unsaved changes. Messages such as "KNOB BANK B" or "SAVED" fill the bar for about a second.
  • The middle of the screen shows what the page is about. Each page is described below.
  • The status line shows the lowest key's note, then "ARP" or "LATCH" while the arpeggiator is on, "SUSTAIN" (or "SUS LOCK") and "MOD". On the right is the program, by name.
  • On the knob row's rule, a tag says what the knobs edit ("BANK A", "KEYS", "TRACK 2"…). Dots beside it stand for the four banks on Play, the four tracks on the Sequencer and Setup's two pages. Under it are KNOB 1–4, each with its name, a dial and its value. A setting with only a few positions shows a row of pips instead of a dial. The one you turned last stands out for a moment.
  • The footer says what the page's buttons and keys do.

The current page's button is lit brightly. So is LFO while the mod wheel is up, ENV while sustain is on, and OCT− or OCT+ while the octave is shifted. PLAY flashes green on each beat while playing. The keys light while you hold them and when a note arrives over MIDI. Every other button glows dimly, so you can read its label in the dark (Setup, Glow). With a scale, the root keys glow too.

5Playing

The 27 keys play notes from F3 to G5 at octave 0. Press OCT− and OCT+ to move them an octave at a time, and the status line tells you the lowest key's note. A note you're holding ends correctly even if you change the octave before you let go.

  • Velocity comes from the keys at first. Touch is on, so they sense how hard you play them, and they can send aftertouch. Set Touch's Velocity to Fixed, and every note plays at the Keys page's Velocity (KNOB 3), which starts at 100. For details, see Touch.
  • To sustain, hold ENV. It sends controller 64 on, and off when you let go. Tap ENV twice quickly to lock sustain on ("SUS LOCK"). Press ENV again to unlock it, and sustain ends when you let go. REC unlocks it too.
  • To use the mod wheel, hold LFO. Controller 1 rises from 0 to 127 over a sixteenth note, and falls back just as fast when you let go. On the FM-1's sounds, it adds vibrato.
  • To change the channel, turn ALGORITHM. Notes you're holding end first, on the channel they started on.
  • To send a program change, turn PRESETS. Each step sends one, and the FM-1's sound follows.
  • To stop every note, press REC. It ends every held note and sends sustain off and "all notes off" on the keyboard's channel and the sequencer's.

The Play page shows the last message you sent, the keys, and the three ports. Each port has a light for MIDI coming in, and USB and Bluetooth also have one for MIDI going out. Under BLE, it says "connected" while a computer, a tablet or a Bluetooth MIDI device is connected, and "ready" while none is.

6Knobs and banks

On the Play page, KNOB 1–4 send controllers in four banks of four, 16 in all. Press FX or turn SELECT to move through banks A, B, C and D. Each knob remembers its value in each bank, so it picks up where you left it.

BankKNOB 1KNOB 2KNOB 3KNOB 4
A74 cutoff71 resonance1 modulation7 volume
B73 attack72 release75 decay76 vibrato
C16171819
D20212223

To use your own numbers, hold FX and turn KNOB 1–4. While you hold FX, they set the current bank's four controller numbers (0 to 119), and letting go doesn't change the bank. To set another bank, tap FX to reach it first.

Turn a knob slowly to move one step a click. Turn it quickly to move up to six.

7Scales and chords

Press SEL to open the Keys page.

KnobSets
KNOB 1 RootC to B
KNOB 2 ScaleChromatic, Major, Minor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Harmonic minor, Pentatonic, Pentatonic minor, Blues
KNOB 3 Velocity1 to 127
KNOB 4 Humanize0 to 40. Spreads the velocity of the arpeggiator and sequencer at random

Chromatic is the default, and the keys work like a normal keyboard. With any other scale:

  • The white keys play the scale's notes in order, from the root in octave 3. Every white key sounds right. The keys that play the root glow, so you can see where each octave starts.
  • The black keys play chords, the triad on each step of the scale. In C major that's C, Dm, Em, F, G, Am, B°, then C an octave up.

Changing the root or scale ends any notes you're holding.

8Sound

CTL-1 has the 128 General MIDI sounds and the drum kit (on channel 10), from Sonivox EAS. They play whatever CTL-1 plays, from the keys, the arpeggiator and the sequencer, through the FM-1's speaker and headphone jack. Turn PRESETS to pick the sound on the keyboard's channel.

Press EDIT to open the Sound page. It shows the sound that's playing, how many voices are sounding and how busy the processor is.

KnobSets
KNOB 1 SoundOn or Off
KNOB 2 MIDI inPlays or Ignored: whether MIDI coming in plays the sounds too
KNOB 3 MASTERVolume, or a controller number for the MASTER knob to send

MASTER has a fixed range, unlike KNOB 1–4, which turn endlessly. When it's set to Volume (the default), it sets the volume of the FM-1's sounds. When you give it a controller number, it sends 0 to 127 across its travel, only when it moves, and the sounds play at full volume.

Up to 24 notes can sound at once.

9Arpeggiator

Press ARP to open the Arp page, and turn KNOB 1 there to switch the arpeggiator on. While it's on, the keys don't sound directly. Hold notes or a chord, and it plays them one at a time, in tempo. With a scale, one black key gives you a whole chord to arpeggiate.

KnobSets
KNOB 1 ArpOff, On or Latch
KNOB 2 ModeUp, Down, Up-down, Random, As played
KNOB 3 Rate1/4, 1/8, 1/8 triplet, 1/16, 1/16 triplet, 1/32
KNOB 4 Octaves1 to 3: the notes repeated in higher octaves
  • With On, it plays while you hold keys and stops when you let go.
  • With Latch, it keeps playing after you let go. Let go of everything and play a new chord to replace the old one.

Each note lasts half a step. The arpeggiator follows the clock whether or not the sequencer is playing, on the same grid, so the two stay in time. A chord you play between steps starts on the next one.

10Sequencer

Press SEQ to open the Sequencer page. It has four drum tracks of sixteenth notes on the sequencer's channel, which is 10 by default. Each row on the screen is one track.

TrackNoteStepsHits
136 Kick164
238 Snare162, turned 4 steps
342 Hat168
439 Clap123, turned 2 steps

KNOB 1–4 set the selected track:

KnobSets
KNOB 1 Steps1 to 16: the track's length
KNOB 2 HitsHow many steps play
KNOB 3 RotateTurns the pattern, step by step
KNOB 4 NoteThe note it plays. On channel 10, the screen shows the drum's name

The hits are spread as evenly as possible over the steps, which makes a Euclidean rhythm. Tracks of different lengths drift against each other, so you get longer, shifting patterns.

You can also edit by hand. On this page, the keys edit instead of playing.

  • Press a white key to turn one of the selected track's 16 steps on or off.
  • Press black keys 1–4 to select a track (SELECT does this too).
  • Press black keys 5–8 to mute or unmute tracks 1–4.

Turning Steps, Hits or Rotate rebuilds the pattern from those numbers, and replaces any steps you set by hand. So set the numbers first, then fine-tune with the keys. The lights show the pattern: steps that play are bright, empty ones are dim, and the step playing now is flipped.

Press PLAY to start and stop it, always from step 1. Velocity comes from the Keys page, spread by Humanize.

11Clock and transport

CTL-1 keeps a clock at 24 ticks a beat, and the arpeggiator and sequencer run on it. Set the tempo (40 to 240 BPM) and the clock's source on the Setup page:

ClockWhat happens
IntCTL-1 keeps time and sends MIDI clock over USB. The default
Int, quietCTL-1 keeps time but sends no clock
ExtCTL-1 follows MIDI clock and start / stop from USB or the TRS jack

PLAY starts and stops the sequencer. With an internal clock, it also sends MIDI start and stop, so a DAW or drum machine that follows the clock starts with it. With Ext, incoming start and stop drive the sequencer, the Tempo knob does nothing, and the screen shows "EXT".

Touch

The FM-1's keys have no velocity sensors, but under each key is a carbon pill whose resistance falls as you press harder. CTL-1 can read it, so the keys play with velocity and aftertouch, with no change to the hardware. It's on at first, with aftertouch for each note. To open the Touch page, turn SELECT on the Setup page.

KnobSets
KNOB 1 VelocityFixed: every note at the Keys page's Velocity. Keys: velocity from how hard you play
KNOB 2 Curve−5 to +5. Higher means you play harder to reach 127; lower, softer playing reaches it
KNOB 3 AftertouchOff; Poly, pressure for each note; or Channel, one pressure for the channel
KNOB 4 AT sens−5 to +5. Higher means a lighter lean gives full aftertouch

The page shows the last note's velocity and the pressure right now, so you can see what your playing sends.

  • Where you press matters as much as how hard. Press near the bottom edge of a key and it plays softer than in the middle, on every key. It's the most reliable way to play quietly.
  • The keys sense in two zones. F3 to C5 read how hard the key lands, with the widest range, and sense aftertouch too. The top seven keys read it another way. Their soft end is narrower, firm presses all come out near the top, and they have no aftertouch.
  • A note plays about 5 ms after the key goes down, once its velocity is read.
  • Aftertouch counts from where the key comes to rest after the note, so a key you only hold sends nothing. Press harder to send more, and ease off to send less.
  • Poly aftertouch goes to each note of a chord key. While the arpeggiator is on, aftertouch goes to the whole channel.
  • The arpeggiator and the sequencer keep the Keys page's Velocity, spread by Humanize.
  • On the Sequencer page, the keys edit steps as usual, the moment they go down.
  • A lit LED changes the reading of the keys beside it. So while you hold a key, the lights in its column go dark each time it's read, and they look slightly dimmer.

Bluetooth

To open the Bluetooth page, turn SELECT twice on the Setup page. The top half is for a computer, phone or tablet that connects to the FM-1, and it says whether one is connected. For how to connect, see section 13. The bottom half finds Bluetooth MIDI devices for CTL-1 to connect to itself, such as a synth, a pedal or a controller.

  1. Put the device in its pairing mode. It appears in the list, with bars for its signal.
  2. Turn KNOB 1 to pick it, and press SAVE. The top bar says CONNECTING, then CONNECTED.
  3. Press SAVE again to let it go.

Everything CTL-1 plays goes to the device and to a connected computer alike. Turn KNOB 4 to switch Bluetooth off or on. CTL-1 starts with it the way you left it.

13MIDI

  • Over USB, CTL-1 is a class-compliant MIDI device called "CTL-1 FM-1". Everything CTL-1 plays goes out here, and MIDI comes in.
  • Over Bluetooth, it's a Bluetooth MIDI device called "FM-1", on from the moment CTL-1 starts. Everything CTL-1 plays goes out here too, and MIDI comes in. What arrives over USB is passed on to Bluetooth, and what arrives over Bluetooth is passed on to USB.
  • The TRS jack is MIDI in only, since the FM-1 has no MIDI out jack. What arrives is passed on to USB and Bluetooth.
  • MIDI in, from any of them, lights the keys and drives the clock when it's set to Ext. It also plays the FM-1's sounds, unless the Sound page says Ignored.

To connect over Bluetooth on a Mac, open Audio MIDI Setup and choose Window > Show MIDI Studio. Click the Bluetooth button, then click Connect next to FM-1. On an iPad or iPhone, connect from a music app's Bluetooth MIDI settings (in GarageBand, Settings > Advanced > Bluetooth MIDI Devices). The top bar says COMPUTER CONNECTED, the Play page's BLE port says "connected", and the Setup page counts what has passed.

CTL-1 sends these messages, on the keyboard's channel unless noted:

ActionMessage
KeyNote on (velocity from the Keys page, or from the key with Touch on), note off
Pressing a held key (Touch)Poly aftertouch for each of its notes, or channel aftertouch
KNOB 1–4 on PlayControl change: the bank's controller
MASTERControl change: the Sound page's controller (when it has one)

CTL-1 passes on channel messages and real-time messages (clock, start, stop), but not SysEx.

14Saving

CTL-1 saves by itself about four seconds after a change, once nothing is held and the sequencer is stopped. Saving pauses the FM-1 for a moment, so it waits until you stop playing. A dot at the top right means there are unsaved changes. Press SAVE to save right away.

Everything on the pages is saved: the knob banks and their values, the scale, sound, arpeggiator and sequencer tracks, the channel, program, tempo and octave, and Setup.

15Troubleshooting

To put the FM-1 in update mode, hold REC while you switch it on. If you installed m-bootloader with the web installer, the installer can reach the FM-1 from there, and holding PLAY instead opens m-bootloader's recovery menu. Without m-bootloader, REC starts the chip's own update mode, which only the rescue below can reach.

If a button or knob does the wrong thing, calibrate the panel. Hold OCT− and OCT+ while you switch the FM-1 on, and the screen asks you to press each button and turn each knob in turn.

If CTL-1 crashes, it shows a red CRASH screen and restarts by itself a few seconds later. If you report the problem, include the numbers on that screen (a photo is easiest) and what you were doing.

If CTL-1 crashes twice in a row while starting, it starts in SAFE MODE instead. There's no sound, but USB works, so the web installer can reach it. Reinstall CTL-1, or put the stock firmware back from the same page. Press PLAY to try starting CTL-1 normally again. If even safe mode fails twice, the FM-1 drops into the chip's own update mode, and only the rescue below can reach it.

If CTL-1 ever crashes while Bluetooth is running, the next start leaves Bluetooth off and says "BLUETOOTH OFF AFTER A CRASH". Everything else works. To start Bluetooth again, press SAVE on the Bluetooth page, or hold SAVE on the Setup page. It starts by itself again at the next restart.

If a Mac won't connect over Bluetooth, check Audio MIDI Setup's Bluetooth window after you click Connect. If it doesn't show FM-1 as connected, quit Audio MIDI Setup (⌘Q), open it again and click Connect once more. Audio MIDI Setup can lose touch with the Mac's MIDI service, and then it links to the FM-1 without starting its MIDI. When that happens, the Bluetooth page says "connected", with "No MIDI yet" in red under it. While it lasts, no other computer or tablet can find the FM-1.

If the computer shows another name, it may be a Mac that knew the FM-1 with other firmware and kept the old name. Remove the FM-1 in Audio MIDI Setup (Window > Show MIDI Studio) and plug it in again.

Recovering an FM-1 that won't start

An FM-1 in the chip's own update mode shows a blank screen, and neither the web installer nor M-VAVE's updater can see it. A script can put M-VAVE's firmware back, on a Mac, with the USB cable you already have.

Open Terminal, paste this line and press Return:

bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/main/tools/fm1_rescue.sh)"
  1. If a window asks to install the command line developer tools, click Install. When it's finished, paste the line again.
  2. If it asks for a password, type your Mac password (nothing shows as you type) and press Return.
  3. When it says it's waiting, plug the FM-1 in and switch it on. If it starts normally instead, switch it off, then hold REC while you switch it back on. The script catches it and checks the chip. It saves a backup of the FM-1's memory in the fm1-rescue folder in your home folder, and asks before it writes anything.
  4. Type yes and press Return. Don't unplug anything until it says it's restarting the FM-1. The FM-1 then starts into its original firmware, and you can install CTL-1 again from there.

The script downloads M-VAVE's FM-1 firmware (V16 and V15) and JieLi's flash loader, and checks them against known fingerprints. It writes only the firmware area of the memory, never the bootloader or the FM-1's own settings, and it checks everything it writes. Running it again is always safe. If it fails, copy everything in the Terminal window and open an issue. If the FM-1 never shows up at all, Felucca's FM-1-transporter can still reach it. It's a small RP2040 board wired to the USB lines.
